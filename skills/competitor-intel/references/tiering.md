# Tiering

| Tier | Criteria | Typical signals |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Market leaders | Large funding, thousands of customers, heavy review volume (100+ on G2 or Capterra), national or global brand |
| Medium | Established mid-size products | Real traction, tens of reviews, a clear segment, not the default choice |
| Peer | At our level | Early-stage or niche, few reviews, Product Hunt or app-store launches, often vertical or local |

## How to choose
1. Ask the user for must-include competitors first.
2. Search "<category> software <market>", "<category> alternatives", "best <category> for <segment>", G2 and Capterra category pages, Product Hunt and app-store searches.
3. Pick 5–6 per tier. At least 2 per tier must be relevant to the user's own market (local payments, language, regulation, channels). For a global product, any direct competitor counts.
4. A competitor can sit in two tiers when the angle differs; say so in the subtitle.
5. If the product is itself a market leader, there is no one "at our level": use Peer for open-source, niche and fast-rising challengers, and say so in the subtitle.
6. Reuse the previous run's list on a refresh unless the market has moved.

Example (Clinozy, India clinic SaaS, 2026-10-31):
- Top: Zenoti, Practo, Pabau, Aesthetic Record, Boulevard
- Medium: Clinicea, HealthPlix, MocDoc, Eka Care, AestheticsPro, Moxie
- Peer: Cliniceo, DoctorsApp, Picky Assist, TeleCRM, Zavis
